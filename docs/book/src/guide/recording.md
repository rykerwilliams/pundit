# Recording commentary

Press <kbd>R</kbd> and talk. Everything you do while recording is captured with
your voice — play, pause, skips, scrubbing, zoom and pan, and your drawings — and
replays in step in the preview and the export. Press <kbd>R</kbd> or
<kbd>Esc</kbd> to stop, and a clip appears named for the moment you started.

Undo and redo are off while you record. Tagging goals, marking slates and
ringing players all still work: they belong to the footage, not to the take.

## Choosing the camera and microphone

**Devices…** chooses the **Camera** and **Microphone**. While you record, a level
bar shows the microphone is being heard, and a small self-view in the corner
shows you where your inset will land.

## Drawing

Drag on the picture to draw. There are six pen colours, each with a dark edge so
it reads on grass and on kits. A drawing fades five seconds after you lift the
pen; untick **Auto-clear** and it stays until you press <kbd>C</kbd>. Drawings
replay where and when you made them, and they stay put on the picture rather
than moving with the zoom.

## The inset: size and corner

Each clip carries a picture of you in a corner of its export. In the inspector,
**Inset size and corner** picks **Small**, **Medium** or **Large**, and the
bottom right, bottom left or top right — never the top left, which is where the
scoreboard sits. The caption bar stops where the inset begins, so your words are
never underneath you.

The last size and corner you picked is what the next recording uses, so you can
set it once before a session. **Show webcam in export** (**Show avatar in
export** when you record with a photo) turns the inset off for one clip.

## A photo instead of the webcam

Choose a picture under **Inset** in **Devices…** and the project records your
voice only: the camera is never opened. Your picture sits in the corner as a
circle and swells as you talk — live while you record, and in the finished
video. The picture is copied into the project folder as `avatar.<ext>`, keeping
its own extension, so your original is never touched; **Remove** deletes the project's copy
and goes back to the webcam.

The photo is a setting of the project, not of the machine: every take in that
match uses it.
